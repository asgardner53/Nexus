# The Polymath Advantage

## Visual Editorial Strategy

Status: active editorial control
Purpose: ensure diagrams and images improve comprehension, reinforce the book's theory and remain legible in ebook formats.

## Core rule

> If a visual can teach the idea faster and more clearly than several paragraphs of prose, it earns its place. If it merely decorates the page, leave it out.

The visual system should feel like one coherent publication. Prefer explanatory diagrams over decorative photography.

## Visual style

- Clean, restrained, professional and editorial rather than promotional.
- HBR/MIT Sloan-style explanatory graphics rather than infographic clutter.
- Limited visual vocabulary: simple shapes, arrows, axes, layers and labelled relationships.
- High contrast and ebook-safe.
- Legible on small screens.
- Avoid tiny labels, dense matrices and sprawling flowcharts.
- Avoid generic stock imagery, robots, lightbulbs, brains, piles of books and staged team photos.
- Avoid visual metaphors that oversimplify the argument.
- No decorative divider graphics between sections.

## Recommended visual count

Target: 6-8 substantive visuals across the full ebook.

Not every chapter needs a visual.

## Candidate visual map

### Visual 1: The Polymathic Capability Architecture

Likely placement: Chapter 3 or immediately after the core mechanism is established.

Purpose: show that breadth alone is not the advantage.

Possible structure:

Depth -> Breadth -> Integration -> Judgement

Or a layered diagram showing depth as anchor, breadth as range, integration as connection and judgement as control.

Editorial warning: do not imply a rigid linear sequence if the prose argues for interaction among the elements.

### Visual 2: Epistemic Mobility

Likely placement: Chapter 4.

Purpose: show how the same problem can be represented through different lenses.

Possible structure:

One central problem surrounded by several frames, with arrows showing movement among them rather than a simple list of perspectives.

Key concept: changing the representation of the problem, not merely collecting viewpoints.

### Visual 3: Surface Similarity vs Structural Similarity

Likely placement: Chapter 5.

Purpose: make analogical transfer instantly understandable.

Possible structure:

Two source-target pairs:

- Pair A looks similar but has weak structural correspondence.
- Pair B looks different but shares the relevant relational structure.

Could use simple abstract nodes and relationships rather than domain-specific illustrations.

Key line to support:

Useful analogy transfers structure, not decoration.

### Visual 4: From Connection to Judgement

Likely placement: Chapter 6.

Purpose: show how a useful cross-domain connection becomes trustworthy.

Possible flow:

Connection -> Test what transfers -> Calibrate confidence -> Recognise competence boundary -> Bring in deeper expertise -> Reintegrate judgement

Editorial warning: present as a reasoning pathway, not a validated scientific model.

### Visual 5: Development and Transfer

Likely placement: Chapter 7.

Purpose: show why component improvement should not be mistaken for broad polymathic development.

Possible structure:

Concentric or distance-based transfer diagram:

Trained task -> Near transfer -> Related domain -> Far transfer/general capability

Evidence confidence decreases as transfer distance increases.

Key line to support:

The farther the promised transfer, the stronger the evidence we should demand.

### Visual 7: Borrowed Breadth

Likely placement: Chapter 9.

Purpose: distinguish what AI expands from what remains human-led.

Possible two-layer diagram:

AI can expand:
- access;
- retrieval;
- translation;
- comparison;
- exploratory synthesis.

Human responsibility remains for:
- understanding;
- competence;
- verification;
- judgement;
- accountability.

Key principle:

AI can widen the search. It cannot inherit responsibility for the judgement.

### Visual 8: Collective Polymathy

Likely placement: Chapter 10.

Purpose: distinguish a room full of specialists from a genuinely integrative team.

Possible structure:

Several deep specialist nodes connected through shared language, transactive awareness, boundary spanning and integrative judgement into a coherent decision or creation.

Key distinction:

Distributed expertise alone is not collective polymathy.

## Image policy

Use photographic or historical imagery only where it performs a genuine evidentiary or narrative function.

Possible exception:

- a carefully chosen historical image in Chapter 2 if it materially supports the dismantling of the universal-genius myth.

Otherwise prefer diagrams.

Avoid portraits merely because a famous polymath is mentioned.

## Accessibility and ebook requirements

- Every visual requires a meaningful caption.
- Every visual should have alt text for EPUB accessibility.
- The core argument must remain understandable without the visual.
- Avoid relying on colour alone to distinguish categories.
- Ensure text remains readable when scaled down on a phone or Kindle-sized display.
- Prefer portrait or near-square layouts for ebook legibility unless a wider format is essential.
- Test in greyscale where practical.

## Citation rule for visuals

If a diagram directly represents empirical findings or reproduces a research model, cite the source in the caption and reference list.

If the visual is author synthesis, label it clearly, for example:

Figure X. Polymathic judgement pathway. Author synthesis based on [relevant sources].

Do not present an original synthesis diagram as though it were a validated research model.

## Workflow rule

During Chapters 8-12 development, each Final Brief should include a short field:

Visual opportunity: YES / NO / PROVISIONAL

If YES, specify:

- concept to teach;
- why a visual is better than prose;
- whether it represents evidence or author synthesis;
- minimum labels needed;
- accessibility requirement.

Final illustration production should occur only after the relevant chapter is developmentally locked, so visual design does not freeze concepts prematurely.
