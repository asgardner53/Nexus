## House style confirmed

### Language

Use Australian English in manuscript prose.

Preferred forms:

- organisation / organise;
- behaviour;
- recognise;
- centre where appropriate;
- labour;
- judgement;
- analyse;
- colour;
- defence;
- modelling.

Published source titles retain their original spelling.

### Quotations

Do not force Markdown source files into typographic curly-quote perfection if the ebook conversion pipeline can normalise punctuation safely.

Production rule:

- preserve quotation meaning and source fidelity in Markdown;
- normalise opening/closing quotation marks consistently in final EPUB/print typography;
- do not alter quoted source-title spelling or punctuation merely to match house style.

### Dashes

Use em dashes sparingly in body prose. Existing hyphenated compounds should not be mechanically converted.

Do not use global replacements on hyphens because they occur legitimately in:

- compound modifiers;
- DOI/title text;
- technical terminology;
- source titles.

### Ellipses

No stylistic ellipsis system is required unless one appears in quoted material. Do not introduce decorative ellipses during copy-edit.

## Signature concept treatment

**polymathic capability** — sentence case in ordinary prose.

Use title case when naming the book's formal constructs explicitly:

Lower-case descriptive uses are acceptable only where the phrase is clearly being used generically rather than as the named construct.

### Chapter-level principle

## Punctuation around citations

Current house approach should remain:

- citation attached to the sentence it supports;
- full stop after the closing parenthesis where the citation completes the sentence;
- no footnote conversion during copy-edit;
- Harvard AU author-year presentation retained.

Final reference audit, not P1, owns author-name, year, DOI and bibliography consistency.

## One-sentence paragraph rule

The manuscript deliberately uses isolated one-sentence paragraphs.

Production rule:

> Does this sentence need silence around it?

Keep an isolated sentence when it:

- reverses the argument;
- closes a section;
- carries a signature proposition;
- deliberately controls the rhythm of a chapter opening or ending.

Merge only when the sentence merely carries continuity and the isolation creates unnecessary vertical fragmentation in the final layout.

Do not make this change mechanically in Markdown before viewing actual ebook typography.

## Antithetical stem watchlist

The manuscript often uses:

- The point is not...
- The problem is not...
- The answer is not...
- That distinction matters...
- This matters because...
- The goal is not...

This is part of the author's argumentative voice and should not be flattened.

Production rule:

- vary only where identical stems cluster within a short reading span;
- never replace globally;
- do not use thesaurus substitutions merely to manufacture variety.

## Protected chapter openings

Protect unless a format error requires intervention:

- Chapter 2: "Leonardo da Vinci is probably the worst person with whom to begin a book about developing polymathic capability."
- Chapter 3: "Breadth has excellent public relations."
- Chapter 4: "The most dangerous lens is often the one that works."
- Chapter 5: "Clever analogies are cheap."
- Chapter 6: "The most dangerous moment in cross-domain thinking may be the moment the analogy works."
- Chapter 12: "The book began with a problem created by success." followed by "Specialisation worked."

These openings create intentional variation in chapter entry and should not be standardised into one house formula.

## Protected propositions

Do not alter without evidence/reasoning review:

- Specialisation worked. The problem is that the problems we face do not always respect the boundaries that made specialisation possible.
- Polymath is an identity. Polymathic is a capability.
- Range without substance is not polymathy. It is tourism.
- Useful analogy transfers structure, not decoration.
- Connection is only half the skill. The other half is calibration.
- Polymathic capability is developable in parts, not trainable as a single proven trait.
- Practise the missing capability, not the identity.
- The cost of asking across disciplines has collapsed. The cost of being right has not.
- Distributed expertise is not yet collective polymathy.
- The same boundary that protects depth can also block transfer.
- The polymathic advantage is not knowing everything. It is being able to move responsibly through what no one person can know alone.

## Known production watch items

### 1. Cybersecurity spelling

The manuscript has used the modern closed form **cybersecurity** in several places. If any residual **cyber-security** appears during the final line proof, normalise ordinary prose to **cybersecurity** unless it appears in a source title or formal name.

This is a low-risk lexical consistency item, not a substantive edit.

### 2. AI / artificial intelligence

Use **AI** once the referent is established in a section/chapter. Do not mechanically replace every instance of *artificial intelligence* with AI; variation is acceptable where it improves readability or clarity.

### 3. Team science institutional names

Preserve the formal distinctions between:

- National Research Council;
- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine;
- National Academies Press.

Do not simplify these organisational names during copy-edit.

### 4. 'Polymathic judgement' wording

Where the formal construct is explicitly introduced or referenced as the named book construct, use **Polymathic Judgement** in final production typography.

Where the phrase is simply descriptive within a sentence, lower-case may remain if context clearly does not invoke the formal label.

A final construct-capitalisation sweep should be performed after the master manuscript is assembled because chapter-by-chapter normalisation can create awkward over-capitalisation.

### 5. Borrowed Breadth

Chapter 9 owns the formal named synthesis. Later references should retain title case when explicitly pointing back to that construct.

Do not title-case ordinary phrases such as "borrowed breadth of knowledge" unless they refer to the defined construct.

## Chapter 12 production protection

The final sentence of Chapter 12 is the narrative endpoint:
