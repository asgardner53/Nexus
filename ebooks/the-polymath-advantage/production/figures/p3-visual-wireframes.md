Purpose: convert the five approved conceptual figures into publication-ready, chapter-specific visual briefs.

## Production decision

The composite five-diagram board is treated as a **visual direction study only**. It will not be inserted into the ebook as a single mega-infographic.

The final book will use five independent figures placed where each concept has already been earned by the prose.

Reason:

- better Kindle/small-screen readability;
- stronger pedagogical focus;
- cleaner captions and alt text;
- easier greyscale testing;
- avoids turning the book into a slide deck;
- allows each figure to be introduced and interpreted in its own chapter context.

## Common visual grammar

All five figures should share:

- white or very light neutral background;
- restrained navy/teal/green/purple accent system;
- strong dark headings;
- generous whitespace;
- rounded but not playful geometry;
- minimal iconography;
- one primary visual relationship per figure;
- labels large enough for Kindle/phone display;
- no decorative gradients required for comprehension;
- no meaning encoded by colour alone;
- dashed lines for permeable boundaries;
- solid boundaries for established expertise/constraints;
- arrows for movement/transfer;
- loop arrows only for genuinely recursive processes;
- human judgement/decision node visually distinct from AI/system capability;
- caption statement below figure, never inside dense visual clutter.

Paw-print motifs belong only in production notes/Studio material, not the final book figures unless deliberately approved as a branding device.

## Figure 4.1 — Epistemic Mobility

### Placement

Chapter 4, after the prose has established deliberate movement among problem representations.

### Learning job

Show that polymathic movement is not possession of many subjects; it is the ability to reframe the same problem through different lenses while retaining an anchor in evidence/depth.

### Wireframe

Centre:

**THE PROBLEM**

Around centre, 4–5 distinct lenses:

- technical;
- behavioural;
- commercial;
- legal/governance;
- systems/operational.

One movable sightline/frame rotates among the lenses.

Below centre:

**ANCHOR: depth • evidence • standards**

A short line from anchor to centre makes clear that the thinker does not float freely among perspectives.

### Caption proposition

> **Change the lens without losing the anchor.**

### Evidence label

**Author synthesis informed by research on expertise, mental set, problem representation and cognitive flexibility.**

### Alt-text draft

A central problem is surrounded by several disciplinary lenses including technical, behavioural, commercial, governance and systems perspectives. A movable frame indicates deliberate switching among perspectives, while a fixed anchor labelled depth, evidence and standards remains connected to the problem. The figure shows that Epistemic Mobility involves changing how a problem is represented without abandoning disciplinary grounding.

## Figure 5.1 — Structural Transfer and Boundary Check

### Placement

Chapter 5 near the end of the structural-transfer argument, or at the Chapter 5/6 seam if layout permits.

### Learning job

Show what transfers in a useful analogy and where calibration begins.

### Wireframe

Left box:

**SOURCE DOMAIN**

Inside:
- surface features;
- deeper relations/structure.

Right box:

**TARGET PROBLEM**

Inside:
- different surface features;
- candidate relational match.

Between them:

A strong solid arrow only from **deeper relational structure** to **candidate relational match**.

A broken/crossed-out arrow from surface feature to surface feature.

Below the transfer arrow, a boundary gate:

**WHAT DID NOT TRANSFER?**

Then a small final node:

**CALIBRATED USE**

### Caption proposition

> **Useful analogy transfers structure, not decoration. Then judgement asks what did not transfer.**

### Evidence label

**Conceptual representation based on established research on analogical transfer, with the boundary check added as author synthesis.**

### Alt-text draft

A source domain and target problem are shown side by side. Surface features do not transfer directly, while deeper relational structure moves from the source to a candidate match in the target. Before application, the transfer passes through a boundary question asking what did not transfer, leading to calibrated use. The diagram distinguishes structural analogy from superficial similarity.

## Figure 8.1 — Building a Polymathic Practice

### Placement

Chapter 8 after the chapter has rejected identity-based routines and established problem-centred practice.

### Learning job

Show that practice is recursive rather than a linear programme.

### Wireframe

Five-node loop:

**1. Problem**
Real consequential question

→ **2. Explore**
Relevant adjacent knowledge

→ **3. Compare**
Structures, assumptions, alternatives

→ **4. Test**
Use, evidence, specialist challenge

→ **5. Revise**
Reframe, deepen, discard or integrate

→ loops back to **Problem**

Small boundary callouts attached to loop:

- depth;
- competence;
- evidence;
- specialist input.

### Caption proposition

> **Breadth becomes useful when the problem pushes back.**

Secondary line:

> **Practise the missing capability, not the identity.**

### Evidence label

**Author synthesis integrating evidence on analogy, practice, transfer, calibration and trait activation.**

### Alt-text draft

A five-stage circular practice loop moves from a real problem to exploration, comparison, testing and revision before returning to the problem. Small callouts for depth, evidence, competence boundaries and specialist input constrain the loop. The figure presents polymathic development as repeated problem-centred learning rather than a one-way programme.

## Figure 9.1 — Borrowed Breadth: AI Reach and Human Responsibility

### Placement

Chapter 9 after Borrowed Breadth has been defined and before the accountability section concludes.

### Learning job

Make visible the distinction between AI-expanded reach and human/institutional authority.

### Wireframe

Left:

**HUMAN DEPTH**
- context;
- existing competence;
- values/consequence;
- accountability.

Middle expansion fan:

**AI-EXPANDED REACH**
- vocabulary;
- concepts;
- candidate connections;
- provisional synthesis;
- questions.

A dashed boundary marks:

**ACCESS CAN EXTEND BEYOND COMPETENCE**

Right filter/gate:

**VERIFY**
- test assumptions;
- check evidence;
- seek deeper expertise;
- assess consequence.

Final node:

**ACCOUNTABLE JUDGEMENT / ACTION**

The final node must be human/institutional, not an AI icon.

### Caption proposition

> **AI widens the search. It does not transfer authority.**

Optional supporting line:

> **The cost of asking has collapsed. The cost of being right has not.**

### Evidence label

**Author synthesis informed by research on generative-AI productivity, overreliance and AI risk governance.**

### Alt-text draft

A human-depth node expands through AI into a wider field of concepts, language, candidate connections and provisional synthesis. A dashed line marks that access can extend beyond competence. Before any final action, the output passes through verification, evidence checking, specialist consultation and consequence assessment. The final accountable judgement remains with people and institutions.

## Figure 10.1 — Collective Polymathy and Selective Permeability

### Placement

Prefer Chapter 10 near the formal definition of Collective Polymathy, with Chapter 11 referring back to the same visual when selective permeability is developed.

### Learning job

Show how specialist depth remains differentiated while relevant knowledge becomes usable around a shared problem, and why not every boundary should be dissolved.

### Wireframe

Five specialist domains around a central node:

- technical;
- legal/governance;
- operations;
- customer/human;
- commercial/risk.

Each domain has a solid local boundary.

Centre:

**SHARED CONSEQUENTIAL PROBLEM**

Only relevant pathways connect domains to the centre.

Pathways are dashed/controlled rather than an all-to-all network.

A final adjacent node:

**DECISION AUTHORITY**

Optional outer arrow:

**EXTERNAL EXPERTISE / KNOWLEDGE**

entering only where a gap is identified.

A small visual key distinguishes:

- boundary preserved;
- pathway opened;
- expertise gap escalated.

### Caption proposition

> **Preserve the boundary. Open the pathway.**

Secondary line:

> **Collective Polymathy makes differentiated specialist depth usable together around a shared problem.**

### Evidence label

**Author synthesis drawing on transactive memory, information sharing, boundary work, knowledge integration and absorptive-capacity research.**

### Alt-text draft

Several specialist domains remain separately bounded around one shared consequential problem. Only relevant pathways connect each domain to the central problem and onward to a decision-authority node. An external-expertise pathway can be opened when the team identifies a knowledge gap. The visual shows that Collective Polymathy depends on selective knowledge movement rather than eliminating specialist boundaries.

## Kindle / greyscale acceptance test

Before artwork is approved, each figure must pass:

1. full-colour desktop view;
2. greyscale conversion;
3. approximately 600-pixel-wide display;
4. phone-sized readability check;
5. figure viewed without surrounding prose — core relationship still understandable;
6. figure viewed with colour removed — line style/labels still preserve meaning;
7. alt text read independently — conceptual lesson remains recoverable.

Any figure that fails by requiring zooming or tiny labels must be simplified, not enlarged with more text.

## Evidence/authenticity control

Captions must never imply that author-synthesis diagrams are validated empirical models.

Use one of two labels:

- **Evidence-derived conceptual representation based on...**
- **Author synthesis informed by...**

Do not use:

- validated model;
- proven framework;
- evidence-based framework;

unless the exact displayed model has actually been validated as such.
