## 2. Targeted re-verification of high-risk claims

### Hill et al. 2025 — pivot penalty

Re-verification: PASS.

The Nature paper reports a pervasive pivot penalty across millions of papers and patents, with research impact declining as researchers move further from previous work. It also reports lower publication success and market impact and explicitly notes the countervailing possibility that staying within an existing direction can create diminishing returns and missed opportunities.

Manuscript use: appropriately bounded to science/patenting and not treated as a general curiosity penalty.

Risk: LOW.

### Boh, Evaristo & Ouderkirk 2014 — breadth/depth at 3M

Re-verification: PASS.

The study distinguishes breadth and depth and reports different relationships with invention quantity, technical influence and career/commercial success.

Manuscript use: correctly warns that this is one firm and one innovation context rather than a universal law.

Risk: LOW.

### Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond 2025 — Generative AI at Work

Re-verification: PASS.

Published QJE version uses 5,172 customer-support agents and reports 15% average productivity improvement with substantial heterogeneity. Less experienced/lower-skilled workers gain more, while the most experienced show smaller gains and small quality declines.

Manuscript use: accurate and bounded to the specific customer-support setting.

Note: older working-paper summaries sometimes report approximately 14%; the manuscript correctly uses the final published 15% figure.

Risk: LOW, but recheck before publication because the broader AI landscape changes even though the historical study will remain valid.

### Dell'Acqua et al. 2026 — jagged frontier

Re-verification: PASS.

The final Organization Science paper involves 758 BCG consultants. For 18 within-frontier tasks, AI improved task completion, speed and quality. For the selected outside-frontier task, the paper reports an average decrease of 19 percentage points in correctness across AI treatment groups relative to control.

Manuscript wording '19 percentage points less likely to produce the correct solution' is supported by the body and conclusion of the final paper.

Important limitation already reflected in manuscript: the outside-frontier result comes from one deliberately selected complex task and should not be turned into a fixed map of AI capability.

Risk: LOW.

### Klingbeil, Grützner & Schreck 2024 — overreliance

Re-verification: PASS.

The Computers in Human Behavior study reports an incentivised behavioural experiment in which participants could over-rely on AI advice even when it conflicted with contextual information and their own assessment.

Manuscript use: explicitly says the study does not show that people always over-trust AI.

Risk: LOW.

### Pearson et al. 2026 — reliance on AI guidance

Re-verification: PASS.

Scientific Reports, vol. 16, article 5345, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-34983-y. The study involved 295 participants judging real and AI-synthesised faces with human- or AI-labelled guidance that was correct half the time. More positive attitudes toward AI were associated with poorer discriminability under AI guidance.

Manuscript use: appropriately narrow and does not generalise the task into a universal law of AI trust.

Risk: LOW.

### NIST AI RMF / Generative AI Profile

Re-verification: PASS.

NIST AI 600-1 remains an authoritative cross-sectoral companion profile to AI RMF 1.0 and supports the manuscript's use of governance, testing, provenance, documentation and context-appropriate risk management.

Manuscript does not misrepresent NIST as proving empirical claims about human cognition.

Risk: LOW.
