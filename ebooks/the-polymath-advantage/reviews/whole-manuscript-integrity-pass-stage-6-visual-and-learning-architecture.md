## Executive verdict

The book should use a restrained, coherent visual system rather than chapter-by-chapter illustration.

Recommended final visual count: **5 core diagrams**, with **2 optional diagrams** if the production layout benefits from them.

The visuals should explain relationships that prose has already earned. They should not introduce new theory, create extra branded models or duplicate whole pages of text.

The strongest visual opportunities are:

1. Epistemic Mobility — changing lenses without abandoning depth;
2. Structural Transfer — what travels and what does not;
3. Building a Polymathic Practice — recursive problem-centred learning cycle;
4. Borrowed Breadth — AI-expanded reach versus human verification, judgement and responsibility;
5. Collective Polymathy / Selective Permeability — distributed specialist depth becoming usable across team and organisational boundaries.

A final synthesis visual in Chapter 12 is optional, not required.

## Visual design doctrine

Every visual must satisfy all of the following:

- teach a relationship faster or more clearly than prose alone;
- remain legible on a small Kindle or phone-sized screen;
- remain understandable in greyscale;
- avoid dependence on fine text or dense labels;
- avoid decorative people icons, stock-photo aesthetics and consultancy-process graphics;
- distinguish established evidence from author synthesis in the caption;
- include concise alt text;
- use the same visual grammar throughout the book;
- avoid adding a new named framework simply because a diagram exists.

## Recommended common visual grammar

Use a consistent system across the book:

- **solid shapes** = established domains, expertise or constraints;
- **arrows** = movement, transfer or flow;
- **broken/dashed boundaries** = permeable but still meaningful boundaries;
- **brackets or callouts** = limits / what does not transfer;
- **converging paths** = integration across specialist depth;
- **loop arrows** = recursive practice and learning;
- **human decision node** = accountable judgement;
- **AI node** = expanded access / generated options, never final authority.

Use one typography system, one line weight hierarchy and one consistent caption treatment.

## Core Diagram 3 — Chapter 8: Building a Polymathic Practice

### Purpose

Show that polymathic capability develops through recursive work on real problems rather than a linear syllabus or identity checklist.

### Recommended composition

A simple loop:

1. Start with a real problem.
2. Identify the current frame.
3. Explore adjacent knowledge.
4. Compare structures.
5. Produce / test an output.
6. Seek evidence or specialist challenge.
7. Revise the problem representation.
8. Return to the problem.

Do not make eight visually equal boxes if that becomes too dense. Prefer 4–5 grouped stages:

**Problem -> Explore -> Compare -> Test -> Revise**

with smaller callouts for evidence, competence boundaries and specialist input.

### Visual thesis

> Breadth becomes useful when the problem pushes back.

### Evidence status

Author synthesis built from analogical encoding, practice, transfer and calibration evidence.

### Why it earns a diagram

The chapter is practical but deliberately avoids becoming a step-by-step programme. A recursive visual shows practice without implying a rigid developmental sequence.

VERDICT: CORE VISUAL.

## Core Diagram 4 — Chapter 9: Borrowed Breadth

### Purpose

Show what AI expands and what remains with the human or accountable system.

### Recommended composition

Left: **Human depth / current competence**.

Middle: **AI-expanded reach** into adjacent domains:

- concepts;
- vocabulary;
- candidate connections;
- provisional synthesis;
- questions.

Right: a filter before action:

- verify;
- test assumptions;
- consult deeper expertise;
- judge consequence;
- retain accountability.

A visual boundary should make clear that access can extend beyond competence.

### Visual thesis

> AI widens the search. It does not transfer authority.

or

> The cost of asking has collapsed. The cost of being right has not.

### Evidence status

Author synthesis informed by verified AI productivity, reliance and governance evidence.

### Why it earns a diagram

This is the point at which the subtitle's AI context becomes visually legible. It prevents readers from misreading Borrowed Breadth as synthetic expertise.

VERDICT: CORE VISUAL.

## Core Diagram 5 — Chapters 10–11: Distributed Depth and Selective Permeability

### Purpose

Show the scale transition from individual knowledge to collective and organisational capability.

### Recommended composition

Several specialist domains remain visibly bounded:

- technical;
- legal;
- operations;
- customer;
- risk/finance.

At the centre is a **shared consequential problem**.

Selected pathways connect relevant expertise to the problem and to a **decision authority node**.

Not every domain connects to everything.

Some boundaries remain closed where confidentiality, accountability or irrelevant knowledge require it.

Optional outer layer: external knowledge entering through an absorptive pathway.

### Visual thesis

> Preserve the boundary. Open the pathway.

Secondary caption:

> Collective polymathy requires differentiated depth to become usable together.

### Evidence status

Author synthesis drawing on transactive memory, information sharing, knowledge integration, network transfer, absorptive capacity and boundary-work research.

### Why it earns a diagram

This is probably the most important systems-level visual in the book. It joins Chapters 10 and 11 and demonstrates why 'break down silos' is too crude.

VERDICT: CORE VISUAL.

## Optional Diagram 6 — Chapter 6: Polymathic Judgement

### Purpose

Visualise the relationship between connection, confidence and competence boundary.

### Possible composition

A simple three-zone decision field:

- **I can decide** — within depth, strong evidence;
- **I can contribute / frame** — adjacent knowledge, partial competence;
- **I should defer / seek specialist authority** — consequence or depth exceeds competence.

Across the zones, claim strength narrows as direct competence declines.

### Risk

This could become a pseudo-framework or traffic-light model if over-designed.

Because Chapter 6 already carries substantial conceptual load, this visual is optional.

VERDICT: OPTIONAL, USE ONLY IF VERY SIMPLE.

## Visual placement principles

- place each visual immediately after the prose has established the concept, not before;
- introduce the visual in one sentence so it feels integrated rather than dropped in;
- do not repeat the whole visual in prose after it appears;
- use captions to state the conceptual takeaway, not describe every shape;
- ensure the visual still makes sense if a Kindle user sees it at reduced scale;
- if detail requires zooming, simplify the visual.

## Accessibility requirements

Every figure should have:

- a descriptive figure title;
- concise alt text of approximately 30–80 words;
- no meaning encoded by colour alone;
- line styles or labels that survive greyscale;
- minimum legible text size for ebook rendering;
- a prose explanation in the surrounding chapter sufficient for a reader who cannot see the image.

## Evidence-labelling rule

Captions should distinguish:

### Evidence-derived model

Use wording such as:

> Conceptual representation based on established research on analogical transfer.

### Author synthesis

Use wording such as:

> Author synthesis integrating evidence and concepts developed in this chapter.

Do not call author-synthesis diagrams 'validated models'.

## Visual production recommendation

Create a single visual style guide before generating any final figures.

Recommended visual tone:

- clean;
- sophisticated;
- restrained;
- contemporary;
- not futuristic;
- not corporate-template heavy;
- generous whitespace;
- clear geometry;
- minimal decorative iconography.

The visuals should feel like part of the same intellectual object as the prose.
