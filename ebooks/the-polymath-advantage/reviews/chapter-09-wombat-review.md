# Chapter 9 Wombat-9000 Review

## Borrowed Breadth

Status: targeted developmental review
Verdict: strong chapter; targeted revision required before lock

## Overall judgement

Chapter 9 succeeds as an AI pressure-test of the book rather than a detachable AI trends chapter.

Its strongest contribution is not the claim that AI can be useful or risky. It is the distinction between expanded access and owned understanding, and the way that distinction changes the value of judgement.

The chapter's central proposition survives:

> AI can widen intellectual reach faster than it can deepen human understanding.

The core concept also survives:

> Borrowed breadth is access to concepts, language and provisional synthesis from domains in which the user does not personally possess equivalent depth.

The chapter should be revised, not rewritten.

## What Chapter 9 adds to the book

The chapter gives the Age of AI subtitle a substantive role.

It does not say AI creates polymathy.

It shows why the book's existing mechanisms become more important when access, synthesis and candidate connections become easier to obtain.

Its most original move is scarcity reversal:

> When connections become cheap, judgement becomes expensive.

That line survives and should remain central.

## Wombat bite 1: 'for most of history' is broader than needed

The opening argument about informational friction is sound, but 'for most of history' invites an unnecessarily sweeping historical claim.

Recommendation:

Use a more bounded formulation such as:

> Until recently, entering an unfamiliar professional domain usually involved considerable friction.

This preserves the point without requiring a civilisational history of knowledge access.

## Wombat bite 2: 'orientation cost has fallen dramatically' should remain a qualitative claim

The statement is intuitively and practically defensible, but the chapter does not have a direct empirical source measuring cross-disciplinary orientation cost.

Recommendation:

Keep it as author-level interpretation rather than positioning the Brynjolfsson study as direct evidence for cross-domain orientation.

The customer-support study should continue to support the narrower point that generative AI can materially alter performance with access to encoded guidance in a specific setting.

## Wombat bite 3: Borrowed Breadth is original enough, but do not overbrand it

The phrase is genuinely useful because it distinguishes external access from internalised depth.

It should remain a book concept, but avoid repeating the capitalised term in every section. The chapter is strongest when the concept disappears into ordinary prose after definition and reappears only where the distinction matters.

Recommendation:

Keep the definition and use the term sparingly.

## Wombat bite 4: source visibility argument is strong but needs one qualification

The contrast between books/articles/consultants and generative synthesis is conceptually valuable.

However, traditional sources can also obscure provenance, and modern AI systems increasingly expose links, citations and retrieved evidence.

The chapter already acknowledges the second point.

Recommendation:

Add one sentence making clear that source visibility is a matter of degree, not a categorical difference between 'transparent old media' and 'opaque AI'.

This will make the argument harder to dismiss.

## Wombat bite 5: fluency section risks implying experienced professionals usually detect errors in their own domain

The manuscript says an experienced professional 'may notice' when something sounds wrong, which is already cautious.

Still, the sequence could be read as implying reliable expert error detection.

Recommendation:

Add a qualifier such as:

> Expertise does not guarantee detection, but it can provide more domain-specific cues for noticing when something deserves scrutiny.

This aligns with the chapter's explicit refusal to claim experts always catch AI errors.

## Wombat bite 6: the Jagged Frontier is powerful but carrying too much conceptual weight

Dell'Acqua et al. is the chapter's strongest empirical example and should remain.

Risk: the chapter begins to make the 'frontier' metaphor do more work than one field experiment can sustain.

Recommendation:

Frame it clearly as one influential illustration of task heterogeneity, not as a universal map of AI capability.

Something like:

> The study offers a useful illustration rather than a permanent boundary map. Model capabilities change, and the frontier depends on task, system and time.

That also helps future-proof the chapter.

## Wombat bite 7: 'one of the strongest uses of AI is orientation' is our synthesis, not established ranking

The section works extremely well in the book's logic.

But 'one of the strongest uses' sounds comparative and empirical.

Recommendation:

Shift to:

> For cross-domain work, orientation is one especially useful role for AI.

This keeps the argument inside the evidence boundary.

## Wombat bite 8: verification section is excellent and should remain durable

The decision not to use a single hallucination percentage is exactly right.

Protect:

> The farther you are from your own expertise, the less you can rely on recognition and the more you must rely on verification.

This is one of the chapter's strongest evergreen ideas because it will survive model-version changes.

## Wombat bite 9: analogy generation is plausible but not directly evidenced here

The manuscript says generative AI is 'particularly good at producing connections'. That is reasonable experience-based synthesis, but it is not supported by a direct comparative study in the evidence architecture.

Recommendation:

Soften to:

> Generative AI can produce candidate connections, analogies and comparisons very quickly.

That is observable without claiming comparative superiority.

## Wombat bite 10: 'attention becomes scarce / validation becomes scarce' is good rhetoric but slightly repetitive

The central scarcity-reversal argument is already made by:

> When connections become cheap, judgement becomes expensive.

The three short follow-up lines make the chapter sound a little manifesto-like.

Recommendation:

Compress them into prose and let the main line carry the section.

## Wombat bite 11: overreliance section is well bounded; keep human-in-the-loop nuance

This section passes.

Protect the distinction between nominal human presence and effective oversight.

The synthesis line is strong:

> Human oversight only adds value when the human has the information, authority, competence and process needed to challenge the system.

But consider changing 'only adds value' to 'is more likely to add value' or 'depends on' because the current phrasing is absolute.

Recommended wording:

> Effective human oversight depends on the human having enough information, authority, competence and process to challenge the system.

Cleaner and safer.

## Wombat bite 12: expertise section should avoid predicting a durable shift in expert value

The manuscript says AI can increase the relative value of problem framing, contextual judgement, verification, exception recognition and integration.

That is plausible and consistent with the studies, but it remains an interpretation rather than a demonstrated long-run labour-market effect.

Recommendation:

Keep the claim at the task/capability level:

> As some retrieval and drafting work becomes easier, these judgement-intensive activities can matter more within the task.

Avoid language that sounds like a forecast of the future value of professions.

## Wombat bite 13: accountability section is strong but three examples edge toward legal/professional universality

Leader/regulatory interpretation, clinician/professional responsibility and employee fairness examples are intuitively persuasive.

The manuscript already says details vary by profession and jurisdiction.

Recommendation:

Keep two examples rather than three, and make them illustrative rather than quasi-legal rules.

The chapter's governance claim should remain organisational rather than legal.

## Wombat bite 14: 'AI can widen the search...' survives, but accountability should include institutions explicitly nearby

The sentence is strong:

> AI can widen the search. It cannot inherit responsibility for the judgement.

The surrounding prose should make clear that responsibility is not always a single individual's burden. It can sit within distributed professional and institutional arrangements.

The current chapter does this later; bring the nuance closer to the protected line.

## Wombat bite 15: 'mature' versus 'immature' AI use is slightly moralising

The Final Brief contemplated mature/immature use, but the draft avoids those exact labels in most places. Good.

Continue avoiding a moral hierarchy of users.

The better distinction is between inquiry-oriented use and premature closure.

## Wombat bite 16: Chapter 8 repetition is controlled, but the final practice recap can be shorter

The closing 'anchor depth still matters, comparison still matters...' sequence is useful continuity but slightly list-like.

Recommendation:

Compress it to one sentence. Chapter 8 has already earned the architecture.

## Wombat bite 17: the chapter will age well if we privilege relationships over model facts

Current model-specific facts are limited mainly to the Dell'Acqua study and current research dates.

That is acceptable.

Future-proofing recommendation:

- keep one current productivity study;
- keep one heterogeneous-performance study;
- keep reliance evidence;
- keep NIST governance anchors;
- avoid adding named model comparisons or benchmark scores;
- keep statements about capability as task- and time-dependent.

This should preserve the chapter's relevance beyond the current model generation.

## Evidence fidelity check

Dell'Acqua et al. 2026: PASS for heterogeneous performance across tasks in the BCG consultant experiment, including poorer correctness on the selected outside-frontier task. Verify final bibliographic details at lock.

Klingbeil, Grützner & Schreck 2024: PASS for overreliance under the studied experimental conditions.

Pearson et al. 2026: PASS as supporting, task-specific evidence on reliance and AI attitudes.

## Originality verdict

PASS.

Borrowed Breadth is useful because it describes a real epistemic condition rather than merely renaming AI assistance.

Its originality comes from the distinction:

- AI can give someone rapid access to another domain;
- that access can be useful before it becomes owned depth;
- the gap between the two creates both opportunity and risk.

The chapter's strongest original synthesis remains:

> When connections become cheap, judgement becomes expensive.

## AI hype leakage verdict

LOW.

The main hype risk is language such as 'one of the most powerful tools ever created for exploratory breadth'. This is vivid but unnecessarily grand.

Recommendation:

Replace with a more durable formulation:

> Generative AI is a powerful tool for exploratory breadth.

No loss to the argument.

## Responsible-AI essay drift verdict

PASS, with one caution.

The governance and overreliance sections remain connected to polymathic judgement. Do not expand them further.

The chapter should continue to treat governance as the consequence of borrowed breadth, not as a separate AI ethics module.

## Readability verdict

PASS after tightening.

The prose is clear and contemporary. The main issue is aphorism density. Several strong lines compete for attention:

- widen reach faster than understanding;
- cost of asking vs being right;
- unfamiliar knowledge feels familiar;
- find the doors / redesign rooms;
- farther from expertise -> verification;
- connections cheap -> judgement expensive;
- widen search / responsibility;
- widen question before narrowing answer.

Keep no more than five conspicuously protected/bold lines.
