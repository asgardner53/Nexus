# Chapter 1 Aardvark Audit

## The World Was Built for Specialists

Status: STRUCTURAL AND CLAIM-TO-SOURCE AUDIT COMPLETE
Mode: Aardvark — heavy structural/source engineering

## Executive verdict

Chapter 1 has a sound load-bearing structure and creates the correct problem for the book.

The chapter does not attack specialisation. It treats specialisation as a productive institutional achievement, then shows how accumulated knowledge and distributed expertise create an integration problem when consequential work crosses disciplinary, professional or functional boundaries.

The argument is structurally coherent with Chapters 2–12 and especially strong in its handoff to Chapter 2.

No developmental rewrite is required.

One bibliographic improvement is recommended before final lock: replace the 2005 NBER working-paper citation for Jones's *Age and Great Invention* with the final 2010 *Review of Economics and Statistics* publication.

## Foundation test

### Central proposition

> Specialisation worked. The problem is that the problems we face do not always respect the boundaries that made specialisation possible.

Verdict: PASS.

The chapter earns this proposition in sequence rather than asserting it as a slogan:

1. division of labour and occupational differentiation;
2. accumulation/deepening of knowledge;
3. greater reliance on differentiated expertise;
4. distributed expertise creates an integration problem;
5. the specialist/generalist binary is inadequate;
6. polymathic capability is introduced as responsible movement, not universal mastery.

This foundation supports the rest of the manuscript directly.

## Load-bearing claim audit

### Claim 1 — specialisation/division of labour has historically supported productive organisation

Sources:
- Smith 1776;
- Carollo, Cohen & Huang 2026.

Verdict: PASS WITH GOOD BOUNDARY CONTROL.

Carollo et al. use US occupational data from 1860–1940 and report increased occupational diversity and associations between specialisation, market extent, technological innovation and labour productivity.

The manuscript correctly confines the study to industrialising US labour markets and explicitly refuses simple one-way causal interpretation.

No narrowing required.

### Claim 2 — occupational differentiation grew markedly in the studied US period

Source:
- Carollo, Cohen & Huang 2026.

Verdict: PASS.

The manuscript's use of roughly 800 job titles in 1860 versus more than 6,000 by 1940 is consistent with the study's occupational-diversity argument.

The chapter does not convert this into a global historical statistic.

### Claim 3 — accumulated knowledge can increase preparation burden and encourage specialisation/teamwork

Source:
- Jones 2009.

Verdict: STRONG PASS.

The manuscript correctly reports Jones's inventor/innovation setting, increases in age at first invention, specialisation and teamwork, and stronger specialisation/teamwork in deeper knowledge areas.

The manuscript also explicitly refuses to treat the 'death of the Renaissance man' phrase as a universal empirical conclusion.

Protect this qualification.

### Claim 4 — major invention shifted later across the twentieth century

Current source:
- Jones 2005 NBER working paper.

Substantive verdict: PASS.

Bibliographic verdict: IMPROVE.

The result was subsequently published as:

Jones, B.F. 2010, 'Age and Great Invention', *The Review of Economics and Statistics*, vol. 92, no. 1, pp. 1–14.

Use the final journal version in publication references unless a specific reason exists to cite the working paper.

### Claim 5 — modern specialised science increasingly relies on collaboration/complementary expertise

Sources:
- National Research Council 2015;
- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine 2025.

Verdict: PASS.

The chapter properly treats these as authoritative syntheses in science/team science rather than evidence directly describing all occupations or corporations.

The text then makes the broader professional implication as interpretation.

Source/inference boundary is visible.

### Claim 6 — team integration itself is difficult

Sources:
- NRC 2015;
- NASEM 2025.

Verdict: PASS.

The chapter does not claim diversity or interdisciplinarity automatically improves outcomes. It uses the reports to support the narrower need for communication, coordination and integration across differentiated expertise.

### Claim 7 — disciplinary grounding remains important in interdisciplinary/team science

Source:
- NRC 2015.

Verdict: PASS.

The chapter explicitly says this evidence belongs to team science rather than presenting the competency synthesis as a universal validated professional model.
