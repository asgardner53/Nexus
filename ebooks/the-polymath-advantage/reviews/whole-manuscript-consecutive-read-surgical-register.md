## Surgical Issue 1 — Polymathic capability definition collision

### Chapter 1 definition

Chapter 1 explicitly labels the term author synthesis and defines it as:

> Polymathic capability is the capacity to move responsibly across knowledge boundaries while remaining anchored by depth and disciplined by judgement.

This is now the mature governing definition of the whole book.

It fits Chapters 4–12 because it accommodates movement, depth, judgement, specialist handoff, AI, teams and distributed knowledge.

### Chapter 2 definition

Chapter 2 later says:

> Polymathic capability is our practical organising concept.
>
> By it, I mean the developing capacity to build meaningful knowledge across domains and integrate those domains in ways that improve understanding or action.

This wording reflects an earlier developmental stage of the manuscript. It emphasises breadth, depth and integration but does not yet include the later book-level requirements of responsible movement, judgement and competence boundaries.

### Why this matters

A reader should not receive two formal definitions of one signature term only a chapter apart.

The Chapter 2 wording is not wrong; it is now incomplete relative to the finished manuscript.

### Recommended surgical fix

Do **not** replace the Chapter 1 definition.

Revise the Chapter 2 passage so it refers back to Chapter 1 and treats breadth, depth and integration as the dimensions being examined rather than redefining the umbrella term.

Recommended direction:

> In Chapter 1, I used *polymathic capability* to describe the capacity to move responsibly across knowledge boundaries while remaining anchored by depth and disciplined by judgement. The immediate task here is narrower: to understand what gives that movement substance. Contemporary polymathy research is useful because it draws attention to breadth, depth and integration.

Then retain the source-boundary discussion around Araki.

Verdict: **REQUIRED SURGICAL REVISION.**

## Surgical Issue 2 — Chapter 5 to Chapter 6 repeats the same landing too literally

### Chapter 5 ending

Chapter 5 closes by saying that analogies become dangerous when the target begins to feel familiar, explicitly using hospital/traffic and patients/vehicles, and finishes with the need to ask what did not transfer.

### Chapter 6 opening

Chapter 6 immediately re-establishes almost the same proposition:

- the dangerous moment is when the analogy works;
- traffic can illuminate hospital queues;
- patients are not vehicles;
- what did not transfer?

Conceptually the handoff is excellent.

In consecutive reading, however, the reader hears the same example and the same warning twice within a few pages.

### Recommended surgical fix

Protect the Chapter 5 ending.

Trim Chapter 6's opening recap rather than introducing a new example merely for variety.

The opening line —

> The most dangerous moment in cross-domain thinking may be the moment the analogy works.

— should be protected.

But the early Chapter 6 hospital/traffic recap can be shortened so the chapter reaches calibration and explanatory-depth evidence sooner.

Do not remove the later hospital-flow discussion if it is needed to demonstrate the boundary in detail; remove only redundant re-establishment already completed at the end of Chapter 5.

Verdict: **RECOMMENDED SURGICAL TRIM.**

## Minor Watch Item 1 — Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 both spend substantial time on breadth/depth

This is not chapter duplication.

Chapter 2 uses breadth/depth/integration to escape the universal-genius definition and establish what a useful contemporary account of polymathy might contain.

Chapter 3 then asks a different question: why breadth itself is not the advantage and what depth contributes.

The distinction survives the consecutive read.

Risk appears only in a few Chapter 2 sentences that begin teaching the breadth/depth argument in advance of Chapter 3.

Recommendation:

During the final copy-edit, check whether one or two explanatory paragraphs in Chapter 2 can be tightened after the definition collision is repaired. Do not cut the Araki evidence or the distinction among breadth, depth and integration.

Verdict: **COPY-EDIT WATCH ONLY.**

## Minor Watch Item 2 — Chapter 12 deliberately revisits Chapter 8 practice

Chapter 12's section *Practice, Not Persona* returns to problem-centred practice, breadth, depth, consolidation and boundaries.

This is structurally justified because the final chapter must resolve identity back into practice.

However, it is the one part of Chapter 12 where synthesis occasionally begins to sound like re-teaching.

Recommendation:

During final copy-edit, preserve the identity-to-practice argument but look for sentences that simply restate Chapter 8 mechanics without changing the final synthesis.

Do not reduce the section enough to weaken the book's rejection of polymath identity as status.

Verdict: **MINOR COPY-EDIT TRIM POSSIBLE.**

## Minor Watch Item 3 — one-sentence paragraph and antithesis density increases late in the book

The device remains part of the manuscript's voice.

The late chapters contain repeated patterns such as:

- Not X. Not Y.
- They do.
- That matters.
- The goal is not...
- It is...

Chapter 11 and Chapter 12 use these deliberately, and many deserve to remain.

In a front-to-back read the cumulative pattern becomes audible.

Recommendation:

Apply the Stage 5 test during production copy-edit:

> Does this sentence need silence around it?

Merge isolated sentences that merely carry continuity. Protect those that reverse the argument, close a section or carry a signature proposition.

Verdict: **VOICE COPY-EDIT ONLY.**
