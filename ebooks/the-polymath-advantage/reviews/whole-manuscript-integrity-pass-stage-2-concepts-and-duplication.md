## Concept register

### 1. Polymathic capability

Role: umbrella term for the book's overall developable pattern of cross-boundary intellectual capability.

Current behaviour:

- introduced in Chapter 2 through the distinction between polymath as identity and polymathic as capability;
- decomposed in Chapters 3–6 into depth, breadth, mobility, transfer and judgement;
- explicitly treated in Chapter 7 as developable in parts rather than trainable as one proven trait;
- translated into practice in Chapter 8;
- pressure-tested against AI in Chapter 9;
- scaled through teams and organisations in Chapters 10 and 11;
- resolved as practice rather than persona in Chapter 12.

Verdict: PASS.

Governance recommendation:

Treat 'polymathic capability' as the umbrella book term, normally sentence case in prose. It does not need typographic treatment every time it appears.

### 2. Epistemic Mobility

First formal ownership: Chapter 4, The Ability to Change Lenses.

Current definition:

> Epistemic mobility is the capacity to move deliberately among different ways of framing, questioning and interpreting a problem while remaining aware of the assumptions and limits of each perspective.

Intellectual ancestry is explicit: cognitive flexibility, mental set, expertise and problem representation.

Later functions:

- Chapter 5: makes alternative representations available for transfer;
- Chapter 6: requires judgement because movement can outrun competence;
- Chapter 8: becomes part of disciplined practice;
- Chapter 9: AI can accelerate access to alternative frames but not inherit judgement;
- Chapter 12: movement requires an anchor and somewhere to return to.

Verdict: STRONG PASS.

No definition drift detected.

Styling recommendation:

Use title case when naming the book's construct explicitly. Lower-case descriptive uses of 'epistemic mobility' are acceptable when the prose is not functioning as a formal label, but consistency should be checked in final copy-edit.

### 3. Polymathic Judgement

Primary ownership: Chapter 6, Where the Analogy Breaks.

Current conceptual work:

- tests what transfers and what does not;
- calibrates claim strength to evidence, expertise and uncertainty;
- distinguishes insight from authority;
- recognises competence boundaries;
- weighs specialist evidence without worshipping expertise;
- knows when to hand off;
- reconnects specialist answers to the whole problem.

The manuscript currently says:

> Intellectual humility concerns recognising limits in what we know. Polymathic judgement goes further: it concerns what we do when reasoning across those limits.

And later:

> That reconnection is one of the places where polymathic judgement becomes visible.

These sentences are strong but do not yet provide a formal definition.

Verdict: PASS AS A CONCEPT, FAIL AS A FULLY GOVERNED SIGNATURE TERM.

Recommended surgical definition in Chapter 6:

> **Polymathic Judgement is the capacity to decide what a cross-domain connection can legitimately support, how strongly to hold the resulting claim, and when deeper expertise must constrain or take over the decision.**

This definition is author synthesis.

Why it earns signature status:

It is not merely metacognitive calibration. Calibration is one mechanism within it.

It is not merely intellectual humility. Humility recognises limits; Polymathic Judgement governs action across those limits.

It is not merely boundary spanning. It determines what can responsibly be claimed or decided after a boundary has been crossed.

It becomes especially important in Chapters 9–12 where breadth, AI, teams and organisations create more opportunities for knowledge to enter decisions than one individual can independently validate.

Do not expand beyond this one definition sentence plus an explicit author-synthesis label.

### 4. Borrowed Breadth

Primary ownership: Chapter 9.

Current definition:

> borrowed breadth describes access to concepts, language and provisional synthesis from domains in which the user does not personally possess equivalent depth.

The chapter explicitly labels the phrase author synthesis and establishes that the phenomenon predates AI while AI changes its speed, scale and conversational texture.

Later uses:

- Chapter 12 uses Borrowed Breadth as a pressure test on the signalling value of apparent range.

Verdict: STRONG PASS.

Governance recommendation:

Retain as a named Chapter 9 synthesis, not a book-wide signature construct.

It is useful precisely because it names one AI-era mechanism without requiring the entire book to revolve around AI.

### 5. Collective Polymathy

Primary ownership: Chapter 10.

Core distinction:

> Distributed expertise is not yet collective polymathy.

The concept names the mobilisation and integration of differentiated specialist depth across knowledge boundaries rather than simple team diversity or collective intelligence.

Later role:

- Chapter 11 provides the organisational conditions that make such team-level integration more or less possible;
- Chapter 12 uses distributed expertise to dissolve the heroic individual model.

Verdict: STRONG PASS.

No definition drift detected.

### 6. selective permeability

Primary ownership: Chapter 11.

Role:

A design principle for allowing relevant knowledge to cross organisational boundaries while preserving the value of boundaries that protect depth, confidentiality, execution and accountability.

The Chapter 11 Wombat review correctly required explicit ancestry and differentiation from knowledge management, absorptive capacity, knowledge transfer, boundary spanning and knowledge integration.

Verdict: PASS AS LIGHTLY BRANDED AUTHOR SYNTHESIS.

Governance recommendation:

Do not promote to signature-concept status.

Prefer lower case in running prose after first introduction. This is a chapter-level organising principle, not a fifth major construct.

## Phrase-level risks to inspect in final copy-edit

The manuscript deliberately uses concise bold propositions. The final copy-edit should search for accidental over-frequency in constructions such as:

- 'The point is not...'
- 'That distinction matters...'
- 'This matters because...'
- 'The problem is not...'
- 'The answer is not...'
- 'It is not...'

These are not conceptual defects. They are cadence risks across a 40,000+ word manuscript developed chapter by chapter.

Do not globally replace them mechanically. Review in context and vary only where the repetition becomes audible across chapter boundaries.
