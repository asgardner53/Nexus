## Strategic Proposition

- **Topic:** Change fatigue, organisational change capacity and the cumulative human and operational load of continuous transformation.
- **Professional tension or shift:** Organisations are initiating more simultaneous change across technology, AI, operating models, structures, policy, workforce and strategy, while the people and systems expected to absorb that change have finite attention, time, trust, judgement and recovery capacity.
- **Central argument:** Organisations rarely experience change initiative by initiative. People experience the combined load. The Change Capacity Gap emerges when leaders govern transformations separately but the organisation must absorb them simultaneously. The leadership task is therefore to govern the total change load by sequencing, translating, removing, resourcing and stabilising change so that performance, judgement, trust and learning are not degraded.
- **Reader promise:** Help leaders recognise when cumulative change demand is outrunning organisational absorption capacity, diagnose the mechanisms creating strain, and redesign the way change is governed so that transformation remains sustainable rather than self-defeating.
- **Why this matters now:** AI adoption, digital transformation, restructuring, cost pressure, workforce redesign, regulatory change and strategic resets increasingly overlap. The problem is no longer whether organisations can launch change. It is whether they can absorb enough change without eroding the conditions required to execute it well.
- **What conventional thinking misses:** Traditional change management often treats change initiative by initiative and frames fatigue as resistance, communication failure or a resilience problem. Employees and managers experience the cumulative portfolio. The system can therefore be overloaded even when each individual initiative appears reasonable.
- **Distinctive concept or framework:** **The Change Capacity Gap** — the difference between the total change load imposed on an organisation and the capacity available to absorb, integrate and sustain that change without degrading judgement, execution, trust or learning.

### Governing idea

> Leaders manage change as initiatives. People experience change as cumulative load.

### Emerging operating model

The book will develop five provisional mechanisms:

1. **Accumulation** — change is added faster than old work, priorities or behaviours are removed.
2. **Collision** — individually reasonable initiatives compete for the same people, attention, time, resources and decision capacity.
3. **Translation load** — managers become the point where strategic intent, operational reality and employee sensemaking collide.
4. **Recovery deficit** — insufficient stabilisation time prevents new practices from becoming normal work before the next wave begins.
5. **Performance feedback trap** — strained execution is interpreted as insufficient transformation, triggering more initiatives and widening the gap.

These are provisional analytical mechanisms, not final proprietary doctrine. They must be tested against research and cases before being locked.

## Reader

- **Primary reader:** Senior leaders, executives and transformation leaders responsible for organisational performance and major change portfolios.
- **Secondary readers:** HR and People and Culture leaders, change and organisational-development practitioners, technology and AI leaders, program leaders, middle managers and business owners.
- **Reader context:** Leading organisations in which multiple transformation demands coexist and where change is increasingly continuous rather than episodic.
- **Reader problem or aspiration:** The organisation needs to change, often urgently, but repeated and overlapping initiatives are creating slower execution, managerial overload, employee cynicism, reduced trust and unstable adoption.
- **Existing beliefs to challenge:** More change is always evidence of greater ambition; communication fixes change fatigue; resistance is primarily an attitude problem; employees need greater resilience; each initiative can be governed independently; transformation capacity is simply a function of project resources.
- **Change in thinking the book should create:** Leaders should see organisational change capacity as a shared, governable system constraint. Good transformation leadership includes deciding what not to change, what to stop, what to sequence, what to stabilise and where capacity must be rebuilt before more demand is added.

## Scope

- **Core themes:** Change fatigue; organisational absorption capacity; portfolio-level change governance; workload and attention; managerial translation; trust; psychological safety; decision quality; capability; sequencing; stopping work; stabilisation; recovery; AI-enabled transformation; workforce impact; organisational learning.
- **Included topics:** Cumulative change load; overlapping initiatives; change saturation; middle-manager load; workforce sensemaking; trust and cynicism; organisational slack; resource competition; priority collision; learning demand; recovery capacity; leadership governance; portfolio sequencing; removal and stopping decisions; practical diagnostic questions and models.
- **Excluded topics:** Generic step-by-step change-management methodology; detailed project-management technique; motivational resilience advice; clinical treatment of burnout; legal or medical advice; broad organisational transformation theory without direct relevance to the Change Capacity Gap.
- **Global versus jurisdiction-specific boundaries:** Written for a global professional audience. Australian examples and research may be used where useful, but legal or regulatory claims will be jurisdictionally bounded and will not dominate the book.
- **Relationship to Alec's other books and thought leadership:** Extends Alec Gardner's work on leadership, human judgement, AI-enabled work and the shift from doing the work to leading the work. It connects naturally with Nexus BMG's leadership and AI-governance positioning: transformation is not only a technology or project challenge; it is a human-capability and governance challenge.

## Voice and Experience

- **Tone variation for this book:** Calm, commercially grounded, slightly provocative, human and systems-oriented. More organisational and executive in emphasis than a traditional wellbeing book.
- **Desired reader experience:** Recognition first — “this explains what we are seeing” — followed by a stronger way of diagnosing and governing cumulative change.
- **Level of technical or academic depth:** Evidence-aware but accessible. Research should strengthen the argument without turning the book into a literature review.
- **Use of humour, stories, cases, and reflection:** Use selective organisational stories and cases, especially where well-intended change produces unintended load. Light wit is welcome; change fatigue itself should not be trivialised.
- **Terms to prefer:** change load; absorption capacity; cumulative change; portfolio collision; stabilisation; recovery capacity; managerial translation; organisational capacity; human judgement; sequencing; removal; change-system governance.
- **Terms or framings to avoid:** change-resistant employees; people just need resilience; transformation theatre; change for change's sake; universal claims that every organisation is saturated; presenting a single score as a scientifically established measure of change capacity before evidence supports it.

## Evidence

- **Research domains:** Organisational change; change fatigue; change saturation; organisational change capacity; occupational wellbeing; workload and cognitive load; organisational slack; sensemaking; psychological safety; trust; transformation portfolios; leadership; program and portfolio management; organisational learning; AI and workforce transformation.
- **Preferred source types:** Peer-reviewed research; primary or authoritative institutional research; credible longitudinal studies; major global workforce and management research; public organisational cases; government and regulator material where relevant.
- **Foundational sources:** To be established through the formal research map. Existing concept families to test include change fatigue, change saturation, organisational change capacity, sensemaking, organisational slack and transformation portfolio governance.
- **Current evidence needs:** Prevalence and consequences of repeated change; simultaneous-change load; managerial burden; effects on performance, wellbeing, trust and adoption; evidence on sequencing and recovery; portfolio-level governance; mechanisms that increase or restore change capacity.
- **Case-study needs:** At least three substantial cases showing different mechanisms: overload through concurrent transformation; successful sequencing/removal/stabilisation; managerial translation or recovery failure. Prefer globally recognisable or well-documented cases rather than anecdotal corporate name-dropping.
- **Claims requiring careful qualification:** Any numerical threshold for “too much” change; universal causal claims; claims that communication or participation always reduces fatigue; claims that change capacity can be captured by one metric; direct causal links between change fatigue and financial performance without robust evidence.
- **Areas requiring Alec's lived experience or approval:** Executive and consulting observations about how leaders prioritise change; examples involving HR, leadership, AI adoption, workforce capability and governance; final framing of the Change Capacity Gap model and any diagnostic tool derived from it.

## Architecture

- **Proposed narrative arc:** Move from the visible symptom of change fatigue to the hidden system problem of cumulative change load; explain why organisations misread the signal; show how load accumulates and collides; examine the human and managerial consequences; develop a governance model for change capacity; then translate it into practical leadership choices about sequencing, stopping, resourcing, stabilising and learning.
- **Approximate length:** 35,000–45,000 words, subject to architecture testing.
- **Proposed chapter count:** 10–12 chapters.
- **Chapter pattern, if any:** Narrative argument first, evidence and case material second, leadership implications third. Avoid repetitive formulaic chapter templates.
- **Recurring ideas or motifs:** “The organisation sees projects; people feel load.” “Every change consumes capacity.” “Stopping is a transformation decision.” “Stability is not the opposite of change; it is part of successful change.”
- **Planned original models or frameworks:** The Change Capacity Gap; Change Load versus Absorption Capacity; the five provisional mechanisms; a portfolio-level change-capacity diagnostic; a sequencing/stabilisation decision model. All remain provisional until evidence-tested.
